Related work
============

This file surveys published research and tools related to smin, as
described in smin-requirements.txt, structure-deduction-algorithm.txt,
and minimization-algorithm.txt.  It was compiled in November 2026 from
web searches.  The summaries are based on abstracts, project READMEs,
and similar descriptions, not on careful reading of every paper, so
details should be checked against the sources before being relied on.

Each entry has a citation, a link, a short summary, and a note on how it
relates to smin ("Relation:").


Overview
--------

The general problem smin solves is usually called "test-case reduction",
"input minimization", or (for programs) "program reduction".  The
literature descends from Delta Debugging (Zeller and Hildebrandt), and
splits roughly into:

* Flat, list-based algorithms (ddmin and its successors), which treat
  the input as a sequence of lines, bytes, or tokens.

* Tree-based algorithms (HDD, Perses, and successors), which parse the
  input with a grammar and reduce the parse tree.

* Language-specific reducers (C-Reduce, llvm-reduce, ddSMT, J-Reduce),
  which apply semantic transformations for one language.

* Grammar-free heuristic tools (delta/topformflat, Lithium, Shrink Ray,
  structureshrink), which get some structure from cheap heuristics.

smin sits between the second and fourth groups: it reduces a tree, like
HDD and Perses, but deduces that tree heuristically from tokens,
delimiters, and indentation rather than from a grammar.  The closest
precedents for that are topformflat (brace nesting levels), Shrink Ray
(bracket balancing), and structureshrink (repeated substrings).  I did
not find published work that uses indentation as a structural signal for
reduction, nor a tree-based reducer whose explicit design goal is
minimizing total test time under a cost model proportional to input
size, although Perses, Pardis, and WDD each address part of that.


1. Foundations
--------------

1.1 Delta Debugging (ddmin)

  Andreas Zeller and Ralf Hildebrandt.  "Simplifying and Isolating
  Failure-Inducing Input."  IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering
  28(2):183-200, February 2002.  DOI 10.1109/32.988498.
  https://www.st.cs.uni-saarland.de/papers/tse2002/tse2002.pdf

  Introduces ddmin, which splits the input into n chunks, tries
  removing each chunk (and each complement), and increases n when
  nothing works.  Defines 1-minimality: removing any single element
  makes the test fail.  Worst case is quadratic in the number of tests.

  Relation: The origin of the "acceptance test" framing, the
  coarse-to-fine granularity schedule, and the 1-minimality goal in
  smin-requirements.txt.  ddmin operates on a flat list; smin's MA1
  granularity sequence G = S, S/2, S/4, ... is a tree-aware analogue
  of ddmin's chunk refinement.


2. Tree-based (hierarchical) reduction
--------------------------------------

2.1 Hierarchical Delta Debugging (HDD)

  Ghassan Misherghi and Zhendong Su.  "HDD: Hierarchical Delta
  Debugging."  ICSE 2006, pp. 142-151.
  https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~su/publications/icse06-hdd.pdf

  Parses the input with a grammar and runs ddmin on one tree level at a
  time, from the root down.  Removed nodes are replaced by the smallest
  syntactically valid fragment for their nonterminal.  Reports simpler
  outputs and orders of magnitude fewer tests than flat ddmin.

  Relation: The most direct ancestor of smin's approach.  MA2's
  level-by-level passes (one per tree depth) are structurally similar
  to HDD's level-by-level ddmin.  smin's "non-trivial candidate
  replacement" (e.g., ";") plays the role of HDD's minimal syntactic
  replacement.  The key difference is that smin needs no grammar.

  smcpeak:

    The HDD loop sits sort of in between MA1 and MA2.  If both are
    restricted to a single level, MA1 and HDD should behave the same.
    One-level MA2 is different because it zooms in on each individually
    required node before trying larger chunks higher up.

    MA2 has a similar level-by-level structure to HDD, but MA2 re-tests
    the higher nodes, whereas HDD does not.

    This suggests several avenues for exploration and comparison to fill
    out the space of choices in the convex hull of these three
    algorithms.

2.2 Picireny, and the Szeged HDD papers

  Renáta Hodován and Ákos Kiss.  "Modernizing Hierarchical Delta
  Debugging."  A-TEST 2016, pp. 31-37.  DOI 10.1145/2994291.2994296.

  Renáta Hodován, Ákos Kiss, and Tibor Gyimóthy.  "Tree Preprocessing
  and Test Outcome Caching for Efficient Hierarchical Delta Debugging."
  AST 2017, pp. 23-29.  DOI 10.1109/AST.2017.4.

  Renáta Hodován, Ákos Kiss, and Tibor Gyimóthy.  "Coarse Hierarchical
  Delta Debugging."  ICSME 2017.

  Ákos Kiss, Renáta Hodován, and Tibor Gyimóthy.  "HDDr: A Recursive
  Variant of the Hierarchical Delta Debugging Algorithm."  A-TEST 2018.

  Dániel Vince, Renáta Hodován, Daniella Bársony, and Ákos Kiss.  "The
  Effect of Hoisting on Variants of Hierarchical Delta Debugging."
  Journal of Software: Evolution and Process, 2022.
  https://www.inf.u-szeged.hu/~vinced/files/vince2022effectsOfHoisting.pdf

  Tool: Picireny, https://github.com/renatahodovan/picireny (Python,
  ANTLR grammars).

  A sustained line of work refining HDD:
  - The AST 2017 paper preprocesses the tree before reduction (I
    believe by collapsing redundant chains of nodes) and caches test
    outcomes, cutting test counts by about 45% on average.
  - Coarse HDD restricts attention to nodes that can be removed
    outright, trading theoretical minimality for speed; it can be used
    alone or as a preprocessing step for full HDD.
  - HDDr changes the traversal order: it recurses depth-first into
    nodes instead of processing whole levels.
  - "Hoisting" replaces a node with a compatible descendant, which the
    2022 paper reports shrinks results by about 27% on average.

  Relation:
  - The tree preprocessing echoes smin's Phase 6 (collapsing
    single-child groups).
  - Outcome caching is smin's `memoized_accept`.
  - Coarse HDD's "accept losing minimality for speed" matches smin's
    choice not to iterate to quiescence.
  - HDDr's recursive descent resembles MA1/MA2's recursion into a node
    when it can't be removed whole.
  - Hoisting is a possible future replacement strategy for smin
    (replacing a group by one of its children).

2.3 Perses

  Chengnian Sun, Yuanbo Li, Qirun Zhang, Tianxiao Gu, and Zhendong Su.
  "Perses: Syntax-Guided Program Reduction."  ICSE 2018, pp. 361-371.
  https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~su/publications/perses.pdf
  Tool: https://github.com/uw-pluverse/perses

  Uses a (normalized) grammar so that only syntactically valid
  candidates are generated, and processes tree nodes from a priority
  queue ordered by size, largest first.  Handles quantified grammar
  elements (lists, optionals) specially, applying ddmin to list
  children.  Reports results 2% and 45% of the size of DD's and HDD's.

  Relation: Perses's largest-first worklist has the same aim as smin's
  descending granularity: spend early tests on big removals.  Unlike
  smin it needs a grammar for each language, and its validity
  guarantee comes from that grammar.

2.4 Pardis

  Golnaz Gharachorlu and Nick Sumner.  "Pardis: Priority Aware Test
  Case Reduction."  FASE 2019, pp. 409-426.
  https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~wsumner//research/2019-Pardis-Priority-Aware-Test-Case-Reduction.html

  Argues that Perses suffers from "priority inversion", spending effort
  on low-priority parts of the tree, and proposes better priority
  orderings.  Also: Gharachorlu and Sumner, "Avoiding the Familiar to
  Speed Up Test Case Reduction," QRS 2018; and Gharachorlu's PhD thesis
  "Accelerating Test Case Reduction," SFU, 2023,
  https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2023-06/etd22465.pdf

  Relation: Directly about smin's stated goal of minimum total time,
  i.e., choosing which candidates to try first.

2.5 Vulcan

  Zhenyang Xu, Yongqiang Tian, Mengxiao Zhang, Gaosen Zhao, Yu Jiang,
  and Chengnian Sun.  "Pushing the Limit of 1-Minimality of
  Language-Agnostic Program Reduction."  OOPSLA 2023.
  https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~cnsun/publication/oopsla23/oopsla23.pdf

  After a Perses-style reduction reaches a 1-minimal result, Vulcan
  applies further transformations (identifier replacement, subtree
  replacement, local exhaustive enumeration) to escape that local
  minimum.  Reports 22-34% fewer tokens than Perses.

  Relation: A reminder that 1-minimality with respect to deletion is a
  weak notion; richer replacements (beyond smin's single non-trivial
  replacement) can go much further.

2.6 T-Rec

  Zhenyang Xu, Yongqiang Tian, Mengxiao Zhang, Jiarui Zhang, Puzhuo
  Liu, Yu Jiang, and Chengnian Sun.  "T-Rec: Fine-Grained
  Language-Agnostic Program Reduction Guided by Lexical Syntax."  ACM
  TOSEM 34(2), Article 34, 2025 (presented at ICSE 2025, journal-first).
  DOI 10.1145/3690631.
  https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~cnsun/public/publication/tosem24-trec/tosem24-trec.pdf

  Uses the lexical grammar of a language to reduce inside tokens (e.g.,
  shortening identifiers and literals), improving Perses and Vulcan
  results by up to 66% and 54% in bytes.

  Relation: smin treats tokens as atomic; T-Rec shows the value of
  sub-token reduction once the tree-level work is done.  smin's
  tokenizer is a natural place to hang such a pass.

2.7 GTR

  Satia Herfert, Jibesh Patra, and Michael Pradel.  "Automatically
  Reducing Tree-Structured Test Inputs."  ASE 2017, pp. 861-871.  DOI
  10.1109/ASE.2017.8115697.
  Tool: https://github.com/sherfert/GTR

  Language-independent reduction of trees that combines deletion with
  tree transformations (e.g., replacing a node by a descendant), and
  learns from example trees which transformations are worthwhile.

  Relation: Another grammar-light tree reducer; its learned
  transformations are an alternative to smin's fixed, language-supplied
  non-trivial replacement.

2.8 treereduce

  Langston Barrett.  treereduce.
  https://github.com/langston-barrett/treereduce
  (Rust; crates.io has per-language variants such as treereduce-java.)

  A fast, parallel, syntax-aware reducer built on tree-sitter grammars.

  Relation: Gets its tree from tree-sitter, an error-tolerant parser,
  which partially addresses smin's robustness requirement but still
  needs a grammar per language.


3. Improved list-reduction algorithms
-------------------------------------

These operate on a flat list, but tree reducers (HDD, Perses) call them
on each node's children, so they are applicable to how smin chooses
which runs of siblings to try.

3.1 Practical improvements to ddmin (Picire)

  Renáta Hodován and Ákos Kiss.  "Practical Improvements to the
  Minimizing Delta Debugging Algorithm."  ICSOFT 2016, pp. 241-248.
  DOI 10.5220/0005988602410248.
  https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2016/59886/59886.pdf
  Tool: Picire, https://github.com/renatahodovan/picire

  Reorders and prunes ddmin's subset/complement tests, and parallelizes
  them; reports reaching 1-minimal results in one-fourth to one-fifth
  of the time.

3.2 ProbDD and CDD

  Guancheng Wang, Ruobing Shen, Junjie Chen, Yingfei Xiong, and Lu
  Zhang.  "Probabilistic Delta Debugging."  ESEC/FSE 2021 (Distinguished
  Paper).  DOI 10.1145/3468264.3468625.
  https://xiongyingfei.github.io/papers/FSE21a.pdf

  "A Probabilistic Delta Debugging Approach for Abstract Syntax Trees."
  ISSRE 2023.  https://xiongyingfei.github.io/papers/ISSRE23.pdf

  Mengxiao Zhang, Zhenyang Xu, Yongqiang Tian, Xinru Cheng, and
  Chengnian Sun.  "Toward a Better Understanding of Probabilistic Delta
  Debugging."  ICSE 2025.  https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04735

  ProbDD keeps, for each element, an estimated probability that it is
  needed, and chooses deletion subsets to maximize expected gain.  The
  ICSE 2025 paper shows that a much simpler counter-based variant
  (CDD) performs the same, and that ProbDD's randomness doesn't matter.

  Relation: MA2's adaptive granularity (double on ACCEPT, halve on
  REJECT) is a simple adaptive scheme in the same spirit as CDD's
  counters: both adjust subset size based on recent outcomes.  CDD
  would be a good baseline to compare against MA2.

3.3 Weighted Delta Debugging (WDD)

  Xintong Zhou, Zhenyang Xu, Mengxiao Zhang, Yongqiang Tian, and
  Chengnian Sun.  "WDD: Weighted Delta Debugging."  ICSE 2025, pp.
  1592-1603.  https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19410

  Weights each list element by the size of the input fragment it
  represents (e.g., a subtree's token count), and partitions by weight
  instead of by element count.  Reports about 10-12% less time and
  smaller results when plugged into HDD and Perses.

  Relation: Very close to smin's MA1, which measures node size in live
  tokens and forms runs by total size rather than by count.  MA2, in
  contrast, uses child counts; WDD's results suggest weighting MA2's
  granularity by size might help.


4. Dependency-aware reduction
-----------------------------

4.1 J-Reduce and Logical Bytecode Reduction

  Christian Gram Kalhauge and Jens Palsberg.  "Binary Reduction of
  Dependency Graphs."  ESEC/FSE 2019, pp. 556-566.  DOI
  10.1145/3338906.3338956.

  Christian Gram Kalhauge and Jens Palsberg.  "Logical Bytecode
  Reduction."  PLDI 2021.

  Model the input as a dependency graph and only remove dependency-
  closed sets of items, which makes reduction of Java bytecode much
  faster (12x vs. delta debugging) and smaller.

4.2 DRReduce

  Qiong Feng, Xiaotian Ma, Yongqiang Tian, Wei Song, and Peng Liang.
  "DRReduce: Enhancing Syntax-Guided Program Reduction with Dependency
  Reconstruction."  arXiv:2605.19412, May 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19412

  Adds a lightweight semantic dependency graph to a Perses-style
  reducer, and repairs dependencies broken by deletions so more
  candidates pass; reports results comparable to C-Reduce for C.

  Relation (both): minimization-algorithm.txt notes that working
  backwards is "a very crude way to avoid breaking decl-ref edges" and
  that explicitly representing such edges is a possible extension.
  These papers are the main prior work on that extension.


5. Language-specific reducers
-----------------------------

5.1 C-Reduce and C-Vise

  John Regehr, Yang Chen, Pascal Cuoq, Eric Eide, Chucky Ellison, and
  Xuejun Yang.  "Test-Case Reduction for C Compiler Bugs."  PLDI 2012,
  pp. 335-346.
  https://fsl.cs.illinois.edu/publications/regehr-chen-cuoq-eide-ellison-yang-pldi-2012.html
  Blog series: John Regehr, "Design and Evolution of C-Reduce,"
  https://blog.regehr.org/archives/1678
  Tools: C-Reduce, https://github.com/csmith-project/creduce;
  C-Vise (Python port by Martin Liška), https://github.com/marxin/cvise

  A pass-based reducer: many independent transformation passes (line
  deletion, balanced-delimiter removal, and Clang-based semantic
  transformations via clang_delta) run to a fixpoint, with parallel
  speculative testing.  The de facto standard for C/C++ compiler bugs;
  produces very small results but can be slow.

  Relation: smin's motivating use case (C++ compiler crashes) is
  C-Reduce's home turf.  C-Reduce's generic passes include
  delimiter-balanced removal, related to smin's delimiter groups, and
  an early pass that uses topformflat (see 6.1).  It is the obvious
  benchmark baseline for smin on C/C++.

  smcpeak:

    I tried C-Reduce on my hello-cpp benchmark.  It does well during its
    initial pass_lines phase, but then bogs down trying to delete
    individual tokens.  I wanted to adjust it to only do pass_lines, but
    then I discovered the next bit:

    C-Reduce seems to not be actively developed.  C-Vise seems to be the
    alternative that is under active development.

    However, I tried C-Vise (2.12.0), and it performed even worse than
    C-Reduce, taking about twice as long while having a similar
    size-vs-time trajectory.  Google AI suggested adding
    --pass-group=delta, but that was far worse.

5.2 llvm-reduce and bugpoint

  LLVM project.  https://llvm.org/docs/Bugpoint.html and
  https://llvm.org/docs/CommandGuide/llvm-reduce.html

  Delta-debugging reducers for LLVM IR that use IR structure (modules,
  functions, blocks, instructions) as the hierarchy.  llvm-reduce is
  the newer, faster replacement for bugpoint's reduction role.

5.3 ddSMT

  Aina Niemetz and Armin Biere.  "ddSMT: A Delta Debugger for the
  SMT-LIB v2 Format."  SMT Workshop 2013.
  https://cs.stanford.edu/~niemetz/publications/2013/NiemetzBiere-SMT13.pdf

  Gereon Kremer, Aina Niemetz, and Mathias Preiner.  "ddSMT 2.0:
  Better Delta Debugging for the SMT-LIBv2 Language and Friends."  CAV
  2021, LNCS 12760, pp. 231-242.
  Tool: https://github.com/ddsmt/ddsmt

  Reduces S-expression inputs hierarchically with term substitution.
  Relation: S-expressions are a case where smin's delimiter grouping
  recovers the full syntax tree, so ddSMT is a useful comparison point.


6. Generic and grammar-free tools
---------------------------------

6.1 delta, multidelta, and topformflat

  Daniel S. Wilkerson and Scott McPeak.  delta (2003-2006).
  https://github.com/dsw/delta
  Debian package "delta" (singledelta, multidelta, topformflat).

  delta is a line-based ddmin implementation.  topformflat reformats C
  code so that each line corresponds to a construct at a chosen brace
  nesting depth; multidelta runs delta repeatedly with increasing
  topformflat depth, giving a crude hierarchical reduction.

  Relation: This is smin's own direct ancestor (by the same author):
  topformflat's "flatten to nesting level N, then delete lines" is the
  idea that smin generalizes into an explicit tree built from
  delimiters, lines, and indentation, with MA2's per-depth passes
  playing the role of multidelta's increasing levels.

6.2 Lithium

  Jesse Ruderman / Mozilla.  Lithium.
  https://github.com/MozillaSecurity/lithium
  https://squarefree.com/lithium/using.html

  Line- (or character-) based reducer that removes chunks of size
  2^k, then 2^(k-1), ..., down to 1, then repeats size-1 rounds until
  nothing changes (giving 1-minimality).  Documents O(m lg n) tests for
  an n-line input with an m-line result.

  Relation: Lithium's power-of-two chunk schedule, working through the
  file at each size, is essentially MA1's G = S, S/2, S/4, ... schedule
  on a flat list (without smin's tree).  Lithium iterates to quiescence
  at size 1; smin deliberately doesn't.

6.3 halfempty

  Tavis Ormandy / Google Project Zero.  halfempty.
  https://github.com/googleprojectzero/halfempty

  Bisection-based reducer for (mostly binary) fuzzer outputs that
  speculatively tests future bisection steps in parallel ("pessimistic
  speculative execution"), killing tests that turn out to be unneeded.

  Relation: Parallelism is outside smin's current requirements, but
  speculative parallel testing (also used by C-Reduce and Shrink Ray)
  is the standard way to cut wall-clock time, which is smin's ultimate
  metric.  See also David MacIver, "Auto-parallelising test-case
  reduction," https://drmaciver.com/notebook/2018/10/auto-parallelising-test-case-reduction/

6.4 structureshrink

  David R. MacIver.  structureshrink.
  https://github.com/DRMacIver/structureshrink

  A research-prototype reducer for files of unknown format.  It infers
  "structure" from frequently repeated byte n-grams, splits the input
  at their occurrences, and deletes pieces.

  Relation: Another answer to smin's question of how to get structure
  without a grammar, using statistics rather than delimiters and
  indentation.

6.5 Shrink Ray

  David R. MacIver.  Shrink Ray.  https://github.com/DRMacIver/shrinkray
  (also on PyPI as "shrinkray").

  A highly parallel, multi-format reducer combining generic passes
  (MacIver describes bracket balancing among its heuristics) with
  format-specific passes for C/C++, Python, JSON, and others.  Recent
  versions add an optional local-LLM pass that proposes rewrites when
  ordinary passes stall.

  Relation: Probably the closest actively maintained tool to smin's
  "language-agnostic with light structure" niche.  A good practical
  comparison point.

6.6 Hypothesis's internal reducer

  David R. MacIver and Alastair F. Donaldson.  "Test-Case Reduction via
  Test-Case Generation: Insights from the Hypothesis Reducer."  ECOOP
  2020, LIPIcs 166, 13:1-13:27.
  https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2020.13

  Reduces the sequence of random choices made by a generator rather
  than the generated test case, so every reduced case is valid by
  construction.  Contains a useful discussion of reduction passes and
  their ordering.

  Relation: Not directly applicable (smin has no generator), but its
  discussion of pass design, caching, and validity is relevant.


7. LLM-assisted reduction
-------------------------

7.1 LPR

  Mengxiao Zhang, Yongqiang Tian, Zhenyang Xu, Yiwen Dong, Shin Hwei
  Tan, and Chengnian Sun.  "LPR: Large Language Models-Aided Program
  Reduction."  ISSTA 2024.  DOI 10.1145/3650212.3652126.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13064

  Alternates Perses with LLM-proposed semantic transformations (e.g.,
  inlining, simplifying), getting results smaller than Vulcan's in
  less time on C, Rust, and JavaScript.

  Relation: As with Vulcan and T-Rec, a post-pass that could follow
  smin's structural reduction.  Shrink Ray (6.5) has a similar mode.


8. Robust structure recovery
----------------------------

8.1 Island grammars

  Leon Moonen.  "Generating Robust Parsers Using Island Grammars."
  WCRE 2001, pp. 13-22.  DOI 10.1109/WCRE.2001.957806.
  https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/4325/04325D.pdf

  Parses only the constructs of interest ("islands") precisely and
  absorbs everything else ("water") with liberal rules, giving parsers
  that tolerate errors, dialects, and incomplete code.

  Relation: smin-requirements.txt asks that structure deduction be
  robust and "never outright reject an input."  Island grammars are the
  classic reverse-engineering answer to that requirement; smin's
  delimiter/indentation heuristics are an even more liberal version
  where essentially everything is water except delimiters.


9. Surveys and tutorials
------------------------

9.1 Program Reduction 101

  Yongqiang Tian.  Program Reduction 101 (free online book), 2026.
  https://yqtian-se.github.io/program-reduction-101/

  Covers Delta Debugging, HDD, Perses and syntax-guided reduction,
  minimality, the "Perses family" of follow-up work, reducer
  evaluation, and test oracles.  A good starting point for the
  research literature, and a source of benchmarks to evaluate smin
  against.

9.2 Xu's thesis

  Zhenyang Xu.  "Pushing the Limit of Language-Agnostic Program
  Reduction."  PhD thesis, University of Waterloo, 2026.
  https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/7b74bf58-8cc2-4496-b3e6-31f9d16d7918

  Collects Vulcan, T-Rec, and related work.


Possible directions suggested by this survey
--------------------------------------------

* Evaluate smin against C-Reduce/C-Vise, Perses/Vulcan, Picireny, and
  Shrink Ray on a shared benchmark (e.g., from the Perses/Vulcan
  papers or Program Reduction 101), measuring both total test time
  and result size.

* Compare MA2's double/halve granularity rule with CDD (3.2), and try
  weighting granularity by node size as in WDD (3.3).

* Consider hoisting (2.2) and identifier/subtree replacement (2.5)
  as additional candidate replacements.

* Consider sub-token reduction (2.6) as a final pass.

* Consider explicit declaration-reference dependencies (4.1, 4.2) as a
  more principled alternative to working backwards.

* Consider speculative parallel testing (6.3) to reduce wall-clock
  time.
